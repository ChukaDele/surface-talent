Policy

Modern slavery and ethical recruitment

Last updated: August 2026

What this document is. This is our voluntary policy on modern slavery, forced labour and ethical recruitment. It is not a statement under section 54 of the Modern Slavery Act 2015. That duty applies to organisations with an annual turnover of £36 million or more, and it does not apply to us. We publish this because candidates and clients are entitled to know where we stand, not because we are required to.

1. Who we are

Surface Talent is a specialist recruitment business serving the UK surface engineering and metal finishing sector, and a sister business of the EMC Surface Technologies group. Our UK company registration is in progress, so "Surface Talent" is currently a trading name; the incorporated entity and its registered company number will be shown here — as [COMPANY NUMBER] until then — once registration completes. Correspondence address: 67C King Street, Knutsford, Cheshire, WA16 6DX.

We recruit permanent, contract and interim roles, from production and process technicians through to directors. We operate in the UK and place candidates into UK-based roles.

2. Our commitments

These are commitments we hold ourselves to now, not aspirations for later.

Candidates never pay us a fee to find work

You will never be charged by Surface Talent for finding you work, for registering with us, for being put forward for a role, for an assessment, or for any part of our process. Our fees are paid by employers.

This is also the law. Section 6 of the Employment Agencies Act 1973 prohibits charging work-seekers a fee for finding or seeking to find them work, and the narrow exceptions in the Conduct of Employment Agencies and Employment Businesses Regulations 2003 cover entertainment and modelling occupations — not ours. If anyone claiming to represent Surface Talent asks you for money, it is not us. Tell us immediately.

We oppose forced labour and exploitation

We will not knowingly participate in, facilitate or turn a blind eye to modern slavery, human trafficking, forced or compulsory labour, debt bondage, child labour, or the withholding of identity documents or wages.

Work must be freely chosen

Before we put anyone forward, we speak to them directly. Nobody is submitted for a role they have not agreed to be considered for. We confirm the terms of a role with the candidate — pay, hours, location, contract type — so they can make an informed choice, and we do not pressure anyone into accepting an offer.

We will not withhold information that matters

We tell candidates who the employer is before they are submitted, and we tell them what the role actually involves, including shift patterns and conditions we are aware of. Our candidate privacy notice explains what we do with their information.

We will act on concerns

If we see or are told about indicators of exploitation — unexplained deductions, workers who cannot access their own documents or pay, accommodation tied to a job on unclear terms, third parties speaking for a worker, pressure not to speak to us alone — we will act. That means raising it with the client, declining or withdrawing from the engagement where necessary, and reporting it to the appropriate authority. We will not stay silent to protect a placement or a fee.

We expect the same of the businesses we work with

We expect clients and suppliers to comply with UK employment, right-to-work, health and safety and pay law, and to meet the standards in this policy. We reserve the right to decline or end any engagement where we have reason to believe workers are being exploited.

3. Our operations and supply chain

We are a small UK business. Our supply chain is short and consists of technology and professional services rather than physical goods: our recruitment platform, our own software and its hosting, website hosting, email and document storage, an AI model provider, and professional advisers. We do not operate international manufacturing or labour supply chains of our own.

We assess the risk of modern slavery within our own operations as low. The material risk in our sector sits further out — in the labour supply arrangements of manufacturing businesses, particularly where temporary or agency labour is used. We do not control our clients' operations, which is exactly why the commitments above are written as things we do rather than things we assume.

4. Right to work and identity

Where we place a candidate, right-to-work status must be established before employment begins, in line with UK immigration law. Depending on the arrangement, that check is carried out by us or by the employer, and we confirm which before a placement completes. We will not proceed with a placement where right-to-work cannot be lawfully established.

5. Raising a concern

Anyone — a candidate, a worker, a client, or a member of the public — can raise a concern about modern slavery or exploitation with us. You do not need evidence, and you do not need to be certain.

  • Email hello@surfacetalent.co.uk, marked for the attention of the person responsible for compliance.
  • Or use any route on our contact page.

We treat reports confidentially so far as we can while still acting on them, and we will not penalise anyone for raising a concern in good faith.

Independent routes

  • Modern Slavery and Exploitation Helpline — 08000 121 700, available 24 hours a day.
  • Fair Work Agency — the body that enforces recruitment sector rules in Great Britain, and which took over the work of the Employment Agency Standards Inspectorate in April 2026. You can complain to it about an employment agency, including us.
  • Police — 999 in an emergency, or 101 otherwise.

6. What we do not yet have in place

We would rather say this than imply a level of formality we have not reached.

  • We do not yet run a formal documented modern slavery training programme. Awareness of the indicators above currently sits with the people doing the recruiting, and formal training is planned as the team grows.
  • We do not yet carry out formal supplier audits. Our current supply chain is a small number of software and professional services providers.
  • This policy has not been through a formal board approval process, because we do not yet have a board. It is issued by the people responsible for Surface Talent. When a board exists, this policy will go to it and this page will record that.
  • We do not publish modern slavery performance metrics, because we do not yet have a placement history meaningful enough to report against.

7. Review

We review this policy at least annually, and whenever our business, supply chain or risk profile changes materially. The date at the top of this page shows when it was last reviewed.
